About the book

From physical light to generative image.

Overview

Color has accompanied human culture across science, art, material production and technology.

The book traces the development through which color gradually became an autonomous organizing principle.

Its historical trajectory begins with the scientific investigation of light and color and follows the transformation of these ideas within modern art.

Particular attention is given to Sonia Delaunay and the relational conception of color embodied in Simultanism.

The study then extends this visual logic into textile structures, digital imaging systems and the algorithmic perspective of generative art.

Continuity

Science, painting, textile, digital imaging and computation are presented as successive environments for the organization of color.

Sonia Delaunay

Color as an organizing principle.

The work examines how color can move beyond representation and become an active structural element within a visual system.

“ Color gradually becomes an autonomous organizing principle. ”

Perspective

The same mode of visual thought can appear in very different technological and artistic environments.

In painting, color relationships organize the picture plane.

In textile design, color becomes part of material repetition and modular structure.

In digital imaging, visual information is organized through discrete spatial elements.

In generative systems, relationships between color, geometry and variation can be expressed as computational rules.
